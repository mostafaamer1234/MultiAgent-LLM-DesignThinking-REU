Market Research:
The U.S. was entering a recession; people were financially stressed and looking for ways to make additional income.


San Francisco and other tech hubs experienced frequent influxes of visitors due to large conferences (IDSA, WWDC), causing hotel prices to surge and bookings to fill quickly.


Budget-conscious travelers, including students, freelancers, and young professionals, often couldn’t afford traditional hotel stays and sought alternatives.


Craigslist provided a peer-to-peer rental solution, but it lacked user profiles, trust, reviews, and structured transactions.


Couchsurfing.org offered free lodging but catered to a very niche, trust-based community and didn't allow payment or consistent availability.


Increasing consumer familiarity with online marketplaces (eBay, Amazon) suggested that people were becoming more comfortable with transacting online.


Urban residents often had spare rooms, couches, or air mattresses, especially among renters in cities like SF, NYC, and LA.


No unified platform addressed both traveler affordability and homeowner monetization with trust-based design.

Initial Customer Base:
Attendees of high-demand events with fully booked hotels.


Younger travelers seeking local, authentic, or unique stays.


Renters and homeowners with temporary or recurring spare capacity.


People who had previously used Couchsurfing or Craigslist but wanted safer, paid alternatives.
